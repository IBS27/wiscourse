Ask: four directions

Ask is an assistant that reads your Canvas courses and makes changes to your wiscourse tasks, plans and calendar. It can’t change anything in Canvas. Every direction shows the same Wednesday, Sep 23, 2026 and the same four courses. They differ in where the conversation lives and how you approve changes.

APanelLinear · Notion AI lineage

A 400px panel docked on the right. It stays open as you move between pages, and it knows which page you’re on. The page is attached as a chip you can remove, so “this course” or “these” work without typing names. Press ⌘J to open or close it. Changes appear as receipts made of the rows you already know from Home, each with its own Undo.

Shared

Whatever direction wins, a reply is built from the same five parts, with the same rules for what Ask may touch and when it asks first.

What it can touch

Reads courses, assignments, grades, pages, files, modules and announcements, as last synced.
Creates and changes your tasks, subtasks, planned days, notes, done state and calendar events.
Can’t submit, post, message or change anything in Canvas. Canvas events stay read-only.

When it asks first

Anything that deletes, and any request that changes more than two items, arrives as a proposal. Nothing changes until you press the button, and the button says what happens: “Add 4 tasks”, “Delete 2 events”.

Single creates and edits apply right away and show a receipt with Undo. Undo reverses exactly that receipt.

Voice

Short and plain. Course codes, not full names. Times follow the rest of the app: 11:59 PM on a lone due time, 7:00 – 10:00 PM for ranges.

Never states a grade that isn’t posted, and never guesses a due date that isn’t in Canvas. It says so and links the source instead.

Decisions

These directions can be combined. The real choices are where a conversation lives (A or B), whether ⌘K can ask (C), and whether plans are previewed in the views they change (D).

DirectionBest atWeak spotPage contextMobile
A · Paneldocked, right Changing things while looking at them. Ask to move an event and watch the calendar move beside it. 400px is narrow for long, cited answers. Squeezes the page on laptops under 1280px. Strong. The current page is attached automatically as a chip. Becomes the sheet directly.
B · Page/ask route Long reading answers and returning to past threads. Room for sources. You leave the thing you’re asking about. Another item in the sidebar. Weak. Context must be typed or picked. Full-screen list of threads, then the thread.
C · Palette⌘K ask mode Fast one-off questions. Nothing new to learn; it’s the search you already use. No room for back-and-forth or proposals; hands off for anything longer. Medium. Uses the page and any #course scope in the query. Search sheet with the same Ask toggle.
D · In placeproposals in the agenda Judging a multi-day plan against the actual week. The clearest confirm step. Only works when the view on screen is the one being changed. Needs a fallback for everything else. Strong, but only for Home and Calendar. Works on Home; the dock sits above the tabs.
Recommendation. Make A the home of the conversation, with its expand button opening the same thread in B’s layout, so long answers and history are available without a second product. Add C as the fast way in: ⌘K answers short questions and “Continue in chat” opens the panel. Take one idea from D: when a proposal in the panel changes Home or Calendar and that view is on screen, draw its rows there as dashed rows too, so you can see the plan in your week before confirming it.
