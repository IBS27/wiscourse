Grades

Canvas answers "how am I doing?" with a page per course, a hidden weights table and a what-if that forgets itself. This page answers it once for the term, then lets you drill into the arithmetic. Letters A/B/C are alternatives to choose between; the index and the course table mix freely. Same tokens and shell as design.html.

0 · What the page has to do

Rules from docs/overview.html and the schema, restated as design constraints.

  • Canvas's number is the truth. The current score and letter shown are courses.currentScore / currentGrade. Nothing is recomputed locally except hypotheticals.
  • Unposted is invisible. A submission without postedAt reads as ungraded, even if Canvas already holds a score. The query enforces this; the UI never sees the number.
  • Hidden totals stay hidden. When hideFinalGrades is set, list the posted scores but show no total, and no home-brewed estimate either.
  • Weighted or points. applyAssignmentGroupWeights decides whether groups carry a percent. Points-only courses still section by group.
  • Group rules are visible. Drop-lowest / drop-highest / never-drop and omitFromFinalGrade are shown on the rows they affect, not buried in a tooltip.
  • What-if is local and reversible. Edits live in the browser, are marked, survive reload, and reset in one click. Canvas is never written.
  • "How much is decided?" The share of the grade that has been graded is shown next to every total, because a 97% built on 20% of the course is a different fact from a 97% built on 80%.
  • Class context when Canvas gives it. scoreStatistics.median appears as a quiet column; absent, the column is empty, not "—".

6 · Decisions

What choosing between the variants actually decides.

Index: 1A ledger, 1B split, or 1C one page?

1A matches Courses and keeps the table wide. 1B is fastest to move between courses and best for keyboard users. 1C reads the whole term without a click and is the strongest "faster than Canvas" moment; it depends on groups collapsing well.

Course: 2A table, 2B strip, or 2C rail + chronological?

2A is the quiet default. 2B adds one drawing that explains weights better than any table. 2C turns the page into a feed and gives the targets a permanent home; it is the only one where "when" is the primary order.

Where do the targets live?

Rail (3A, 2C), summary block, or a popover on the letter. Rail is always visible and holds the edits list; the summary block fits 1B; the popover keeps the default page free of hypotheticals.

Class median column: yes or no?

Canvas only sends scoreStatistics for some assignments. When present it is the most-asked question after "what did I get". When absent the column is blank, which is fine in 2A and awkward in 1B's narrow pane.

Letter cutoffs for "to finish with"

Canvas exposes the course grading scheme on the enrollment in some cases and not others. Fallback: the standard UW scale (A 93 · AB 88 · B 83 · BC 78 · C 70 · D 60), editable per course. Without a scheme the targets list percentages only.

Current vs final

Canvas reports both: current (graded only) and final (ungraded as zero). Every variant shows current and hides final; the coverage bar is the honest version of the same warning. Final could appear in the footer as "if everything left were zero".
