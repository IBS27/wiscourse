Calendar — four directions

Home already answers “what do I do next”. The calendar answers “where does my time go this week” and “when is everything due this month”. Every direction below shows the same week (Mon Sep 7 – Sun Sep 13, 2026, today Thu 10) with the same four kinds of item: class meetings, due times, planned todos, and events. They differ in what gets the space.

AGridBMonthCAgendaDBoard MobileSharedDecisions

AGridNotion Calendar · Cron lineage

A time grid for the things that actually occupy time (class meetings, office hours, local events), with two thin bands above it for the things that don’t: Due and Planned. Due times never become blocks — a 11:59 PM deadline is not a 1-minute meeting. Meetings are filled with a tint of their course colour, with local events lighter and dashed, so the week reads as blocks of colour on a quiet grid.

Seven columns, no time axis. Each day stacks its class meetings as a compact schedule at the top, then its due items as cards, then the todos planned for that day. Cards drag between columns to set a planned day, which makes this the only direction where the calendar is also the planning surface, not just a picture of it.

Shared

Whatever direction wins, these parts are the same: one mark per kind of item, course colour as the only hue, and the subscribe sheet.

Colour

Course colour fills time blocks (meetings, events) as a tint, and marks everything else. Personal items use the neutral course colour. Red appears twice: the now-line, and “late” on an overdue due card. Exams are events, so they read as rings.

Time

Tabular numerals everywhere. Ranges use an en dash with spaces (11:00 – 12:15); meridiem is dropped inside a range and kept on a lone due time (5:00 PM). Dense cells use 11:59p. Grid rows are 48px per hour, never smaller than 40px per block.

States

Past days lose contrast but keep their content. Today gets the sunken background and the filled date. A due item that is submitted stays on its day, struck through, so the week still shows what happened. Nothing is optimistic here — the calendar only reflects what Convex already knows.

Decisions

The four directions aren’t exclusive: Week / Month / Day are separate views, so this is really two choices — which week view, and whether month gets a day popover.

DirectionBest atWeak spotPlanningMobileBuild
A · Gridweek, time axis Seeing free time between classes. The only view where a 90-minute gap is visible as 90 minutes. Deadlines live in a band, not the grid, so a heavy due week looks calm. Weekends waste two columns. None on the grid. Plan band is display-only; planning happens on Home. Timeline day. Clean mapping. Absolute positioning, overlap layout, now-line. The most code.
B · Monthmonth, popover Exam and deadline overview across a term. Reads like a syllabus. Useless for today. Cells overflow in project weeks; classes must be hidden. Popover can add a plan for the selected day. Dot grid, tap to Day. Small. Shares the popover rows with Home.
C · Agendalist, no grid Scanning. One column, one scroll, works at any width without a second layout. Overlaps Home almost entirely; the only differences are meetings and the mini-month. Same as Home (checkbox, planned day in the row menu). It already is the mobile layout. Smallest. Mostly existing row components.
D · Boardweek, columns Deciding which day to do things. Drag-to-plan makes the week a workspace. No sense of duration; a 15-min quiz and a 3-hour lab are the same line. Seven narrow columns wrap titles. Native. Drag between columns, drop from Tasks. List day with the same groups. Medium. Drag-and-drop and a todos mutation for plannedDay.
Recommendation. Ship A as Week and B as Month, with the timeline Day on mobile. They are the two views Home cannot be, and together they cover “where is my time” and “what is coming”. Fold D’s one real idea in later: let the Plan band in A accept drops from Tasks. Skip C as a route; it is Home with a different header.
